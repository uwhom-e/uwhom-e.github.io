## Overview

We set up a simulator in MuJoCo so we can test control architectures before the hardware is complete.

#### Simulator
The sim (`sim/` in the monorepo) puts N mobile manipulators and one rigid payload in a scene. Each robot is a Stanford TidyBot from MuJoCo Menagerie: a holonomic base, a Kinova Gen3 arm and a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper. The payload starts on a pair of stands, and a second pair of stands marks the destination.

Each robot gets an onboard-style interface:

- **Sense:** drifting odometry, joint angles, velocities and torques, a wrist force/torque sensor, and the end-effector pose. Base-frame views (`ee_in_base`, `wrist_wrench_base`) need no world-frame knowledge.
- **Command:** base twist (body or world frame), arm joint targets, IK reach (world or base frame), gripper, and an optional rigid (weld) grasp.
- **Ground truth** is kept separate and used only for scoring.

The rule is that algorithms only call `Robot` methods. That way the same code moves to the ROS 2 bridge and then to hardware.

The rest of the package:

- **`Sim`** steps the scene at a fixed control rate, opens the viewer, records MP4 video on sim time, and stops on divergence instead of letting MuJoCo silently reset.
- **ROS 2 bridge** runs in WSL and exposes each robot as `/rN/odom`, `/rN/cmd_vel`, `/rN/joint_states`, `/rN/wrist_wrench`, `/rN/arm/joint_command` and `/rN/gripper/command`, plus `/clock` and ground-truth poses. A setup script installs ROS 2 Jazzy and ros2_control.
- **Smoke test:** two robots grasp the bar ends, lift it together, carry it 0.6 m to the second stands and set it down.
- **Sandbox:** random spawns and numpad teleop of one robot's base in the viewer.

#### Caster leader–follower controller
The first controller in the sim (`ctrl/caster_ctrl/`) is leader–follower co-carry, with followers that act like the virtual caster from Hirata, Kume, Sawada, Wang and Kosuge, *Handling of an Object by Multiple Mobile Manipulators in Coordination based on Caster-like Dynamics* (ICRA 2004).

- **Leader:** the operator drives the **object** with the numpad. The leader turns that twist into a desired object pose and then into a target for its own gripper and base.
- **Follower:** gets no messages. It reads only its own wrist force, joint angles and odometry, turns its heading toward the pull, yields along it, and estimates where the leader is going.

One follower tick:

1. Tare, sign-flip and low-pass the wrist force.
2. Turn the heading toward the force, treating it as an axis so it doesn't swing 180° when the force reverses.
3. Split the force along and across the heading.
4. Along the heading: a mass–spring–damper (Eq. 3 in the paper).
5. A leader-trajectory estimator, G₁(s) = (a₁s + b₁)/s², moves the follower's reference so it tracks a leader moving at constant speed with no lag.
6. Across the heading: damping only, like a caster swivelling.
7. Vertical and grasp yaw are handled the same way (yaw as a damped free joint).
8. Split the gripper target between the arm and the base.

Around this sit a scripted grasp → lift → settle → tare phase machine, numpad object teleop with speed and acceleration limits, a per-tick CSV log with a run summary, and 8 NumPy-only unit tests. Nothing in the controller imports the sim.

#### Results
A scripted test matrix with a 2 kg × 1.4 m bar, rigid grasp, at 250 Hz. F is the follower's internal force (tared wrist force), and pos err is the final object position against the commanded pose.

| Scenario | Stiff follower F rms / peak | Caster F rms / peak | Caster pos err |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hold 5 s | 5.3 / 12.5 N | 0.9 / 10.4 N | 0.3 cm |
| 0.6 m along the bar | 930 / 4162 N, stalled | 1.9 / 10.4 N | 0.2 cm |
| 0.6 m across the bar | 176 / 243 N, stalled | 1.9 / 10.4 N | 0.2 cm |
| Lift and lower 0.1 m | 12.6 / 21.9 N | 0.9 / 10.4 N | 0.3 cm |
| Yaw 45° in place | 397 / 543 N, stalled | 3.9 / 10.4 N | 3.0 cm |
| L-path 0.5 m + 0.5 m | 838 / 4162 N, stalled | 2.2 / 10.4 N | 0.2 cm |

The caster follower meets the pass bar on every scenario: RMS under 5 N, peak under 20 N, error at most 3 cm, and at least 5× below the stiff follower. The stiff follower stalls in four of the six. The 10.4 N peak is the same small transient at the start of every carry.

#### Changes from the design doc
- **Wrench low-pass at 40 Hz, not 15 Hz.** At 15 Hz the filter lag let the two robots ring at about 11 Hz along the bar and diverge.
- **Grasp yaw damping of 20 N·m·s/rad, not 2.** The softer damper went unstable against the stiff arm at 250 Hz.
- **Stiff leader by default.** A compliant leader also works but gives way, leaving the object 3–9 cm off the command.
- **Estimator γ = 2 kept with the stiff leader.** The exact value for a stiff leader is 1, but 2 gave lower forces in every scenario.
- **Heading tracks a 90° turn in about 0.9 s, not 0.5 s,** because of the 2 rad/s rate cap.

## What we did

- Built a MuJoCo co-carry sim with N TidyBots, a payload, an onboard-style per-robot interface, a video recorder and a smoke test
- Added a ROS 2 bridge and a WSL setup script
- Moved the sim to the top level of the monorepo and fixed the weld-anchor bug in `rigid_grasp()`
- Implemented the caster leader–follower controller with unit tests and a scripted scenario matrix
- Showed the caster follower keeps internal force under 4 N RMS in every scenario, where a stiff follower stalls in four of six
