## Overview

Following the pivot, the team worked on scoping the bimanual manipulation project: what the two robots must do, what is out of scope, and how success will be measured.

## What we did

#### Camron
- Scoped the bimanual manipulation project around two identical single-arm mobile robots coordinating as one system
- Defined what is in and out of scope (onboard sensing only, two robots, no human-video learning or voice control)
- Drafted criteria, constraints, and the demonstration tasks for the proposal
- Reviewed papers framing the gap between shared-body two-arm research and multi-robot transport research: [Mobile ALOHA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02117), Khatib et al. (1996), [CHORUS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12352), and [Body Extension by Using Two Mobile Manipulators](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10022877/)
- Searched related patents; the closest each cover one part of the idea:
  - [US11772269B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11772269B2/en) (Dexterity): robots that work alone and team up with a leader/follower scheme, but in a shared workspace with a common camera
  - [US12263599B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12263599B2/en) (Intel): multi-robot tasks built from reusable action primitives
  - [US10456912B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10456912): auction-based task allocation, choosing one robot per task rather than a team
